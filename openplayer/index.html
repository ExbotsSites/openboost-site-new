OpenPlayer

Let the song lead.

A liquid-glass music player for Windows. Synced lyrics, an eleven-band EQ, crossfade, a desktop widget, and Discord presence — built to disappear once the music starts.

The song stays in front.

OpenPlayer gives the track the room. Cover art, a real progress line, and lyrics that move with the vocal — while the controls wait until you reach for them.

Line-synced lyrics fade around the active line, so the next one is always in view. The room gets quiet; the song does the talking.

Shuffle, repeat, seek and volume sit in one compact card. No tabs, no feed, no second screen to manage.

The player gets out of the way.
The record doesn't.

Eleven bands, one honest room.

Stereo or mono. A limiter that stops clipping. Crossfade from one track into the next. And an eleven-band EQ with presets from Flat to Vocal, or a curve of your own.

Eleven bands, custom curves, a limiter, and a crossfade that makes a queue feel like a set.

A desktop widget when you want the player small. Discord activity and online covers when you want to share what is playing.

Your library, not a feed.

Search YouTube Music or bring your own files. Save playlists, like the songs that stay, and import the library you already have. The account is optional; the music is the point.

Local music, YouTube Music search, playlists and liked songs in one place. Add files or import from Spotify and CSV.

Use it without an account. Sign in with Discord only when you want your library, liked songs and settings to follow you.

Take it with you.

OpenPlayer is open source. Grab the latest Windows build from GitHub, bring your library, and leave the account at the door.
